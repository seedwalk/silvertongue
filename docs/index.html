Features

The problem

Your character has been silent this whole time

You know exactly what is happening. The warrior held the line, the group wiped, a stranger helped you on a quest. And then you type ty and move on, because stopping to write something in character costs more than the moment is worth.

Silvertongue makes that cost three seconds. Click a target, pick Praise, read the line, click Say.

Nothing reaches chat until you click to send it. Every button, key and menu fills a box and stops. You read the line first, every time.

One

It lives on the frames you already watch

No window to go and find, nothing covering your screen. A small speech bubble sits on your portrait, on your target, on each party member, and on every row of the group browser. Clicking one opens a menu shaped like the game's own — because it should feel like part of the game, not like something bolted on top of it.

  • Opens beside what you clicked, never on top of it.
  • Click away to dismiss, the way a right-click menu behaves.
  • Drag any of it wherever you want it. It stays there.

Two

It sounds like your character

A line is assembled in layers: a shared pool, then your class, your race and your faction, then the class and race of whoever you are speaking to. Nobody wrote “undead mage” — the undead layer and the mage layer together already read as one.

Three

It reaches the rest of the world too

It bows when it thanks you

Lines carry the matching emote, so your character actually bows, salutes or roars — aimed at whoever you are speaking to. A checkbox shows you before it happens, because other people see it too.

It finds you a group

A bubble in the group browser advertises you on the channel, opening with the shorthand people actually scan for. Every listing grows one too: it reads who posted it, what they are running and what they are short of, then offers the roles your class could fill.

LFG Scarlet Monastery — orc rogue, 36.

It stays out of your way

Nothing is ever said without you pressing the channel. Drag any bubble where you want it, put the panel on a key, or hide the parts you do not use — including the minimap button.

Four

Every word of it is yours

The library ships full, not finished. Open it in game and the whole thing is an editor: no file to unzip, nothing to reload, no Lua to write.

Write, edit, delete

Add a line where you missed one, reword one that sounds wrong, hide the ones that are not your character. Favourites float to the top of their menu.

Choose who it belongs to

Each edit is scoped: this character only, every orc you roll, every shaman, the whole Horde, or everyone. Write once, and the right character says it.

Arrange the menus

Reorder the buttons, drop in separators, and drag every bubble to wherever your unit frames actually are — replacement frames included. Positions are remembered.

Pick the gesture

Any line can carry any emote, or none — bow, salute, roar, chosen per line and stored with the same scopes.

Your changes are kept as a layer on top of the shipped library, never as a copy of it — so new phrases in later versions still reach you, underneath your own.

Install

Three steps, no setup

  1. 1

    Download and unzip it

    You should end up with a folder called Silvertongue.

  2. 2

    Put it in your AddOns folder

    World of Warcraft/_anniversary_/Interface/AddOns/Silvertongue/
  3. 3

    Restart the game

    Not /reload — new addons are only found when the client launches.

The one thing that goes wrong

You need AddOns/Silvertongue/Silvertongue.toc. If unzipping left an extra folder in between — AddOns/Silvertongue-rc-0.1.0/Silvertongue/ — the game will never find it. Move the inner folder up one level.

Which build?

v0.1.0 is finished. rc-0.1.0 passes the tests but has had less time in a real game. Both install the same way.

Updating

Replace the folder. Your own phrases and layout live in the game's saved variables, not in the addon, so they survive.
